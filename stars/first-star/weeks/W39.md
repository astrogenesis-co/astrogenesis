# W39

## Planning

### Overview

Two threads this week, both downstream of last week's realizations. On the work side, the strategy has shifted to demo-first: make one excellent work as an attractor and move from there. On the social side, the next few weeks are a build-up phase toward the fixed dates (Toastmasters October 6, improv starting October 12), so the job now is deliberate practice, not outcomes.

One more thing the demo-first ordering buys: it works professionally. Leading with the code means having a demo — something concrete to show for my line of work — as soon as possible, and I can move forward on the creative stuff from that footing instead of having everything in progress and nothing presentable.

### Roadmap and Timing

The phasing from last week's retro replaces the track-per-week schedule: September for code, October for music, November for writing. The fixed dates still hold — launch Friday November 27, last day December 5.

- **W39 (Sep 21–27):** first shareable demo by end of week. This is a hard commitment, not a stretch goal — placeholder content is fine, shareable is the bar.
- **W40 (Sep 28 – Oct 4):** feedback and iteration on the demo. By the end of September the demo has been seen by real people and I'm in good shape to switch modes.
- **October:** music production as the single focus, with feedback gathered as I go.
- **November:** finish the writing (Essay 0 lands here), apply the accumulated feedback in the first two weeks, then rollout and launch.

October is admittedly tighter than the original seven-week track path — but the honest comparison isn't seven weeks versus four and a half. The old plan split focus between writing, dev, and music roughly equally throughout, on top of the social work. A single-focus October is plausibly more real production time than seven split weeks, and by then the demo and its feedback will have sharpened what the music actually needs to be. If October opens and that math isn't working, that's the signal to cut track count or revisit the dates — decide it then, explicitly, rather than discovering it in November.

### Demo

The one non-negotiable: a first shareable demo by the end of this week. Placeholders for content are fine — the point is having something concrete to show and to collect feedback against, since I need feedback to iterate. It doesn't go wide; it goes in front of a small circle.

To keep the feedback moment from sliding the way Essay 0's did: decide **this week** who the two or three people in that circle are, and tell them something is coming. The pattern to break is that building ships and showing slips — so the showing gets committed before the demo is finished, not after. When the demo lands at the end of the week, the audience already exists.

Supporting moves that go with it:

- Move to a dev server and gate the dev stuff — public interface stays clean.
- Make the existing repo and project private (still used for history), and start a new pure UX repo as the public project.
- Rejigger the Astrogenesis pitch around the new strategy: create a work first. This isn't "build it and they will come" — it's making the building itself the thing you can point to. Probably worth an Astrogenesis blog post on the build-in-public realization behind it.

### Social

The daily experiment: in every interaction — at a store, in the elevator — say one thing beyond what's necessary. Review how it went each evening. The frame from the retro carries: every social interaction right now is pure practice.

One idea to keep warm: a talk to go along with the project that I can start practicing, which would eventually tie the two threads together.
