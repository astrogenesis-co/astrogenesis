# W36

## Retro

### Overview

This week ended up mainly being for some foundational writing and getting the basic rhythms under me. In that sense, it went reasonably well. The one major issue I did identify, though, is a tendency to stand outside the work instead of being in it. That showed up two ways: getting pulled into "get things done" mode, and wanting to shape the process into a clean story as it happens.

### Went Well

My sleep schedule is pretty much on track. Got a bit lucky at first, where my alarm went off at a good point in my sleep cycle. Later in the week I had a few times where I was mid-dream, which made it tougher, but I still powered through it. Getting light right away and jumping right into the routine seemed to help. I feel pretty adjusted to it already, but will continue to monitor.

I also made some very real progress on the first piece of writing. This is a pretty foundational piece, so I am okay with taking my time with it. It establishes where I personally am and why I am doing this specific project. Main thing is I do need a hard deadline for next week, otherwise I could go overboard with it. But it's close, so I feel good about that. I also started some good writing practices. These include the up-front work of explaining the purpose of the piece, the audience, etc., and starting with a stream-of-consciousness bad draft. That is a good place to use AI, since it can help identify answers to those up-front questions from a bad draft. Then just continue redrafting and revising until ready. Was not the intent, but having a week to kind of slip back into writing was good, and that is another benefit of how this week went.

### What Didn't Go Well

One thing I noticed is that I was very much naturally pulled into "get things done" mode rather than being present in what I was actually doing. That makes sense — despite my connection to the work, it still needs to get done in a timely manner. But this isn't even completely about efficiency; it is more that not being present produces lower-quality work in the same amount of time anyway. This also relates to a real attitude shift I want to make towards work through that process. It kind of goes back to the old saying "How you do anything is how you do everything." If I am deciding to do something, I want to give it all my focus and attention.

I also noticed that because capturing the process is a core part of the project, there is this pressure to turn the process itself into a clean story as it happens. I wouldn't say it is quite about being "performative" in the colloquial sense, like I am doing it for an external audience. But it is a thing where I am trying to do the work in narrative form, which just isn't how work happens. There is a story at play from a higher vantage point, but the day-to-day work isn't going to be that clean.

And that is the whole idea of the project: I can capture the work as is, which tells a real story about what actually happened. That is valuable, and it isn't something you can plan out fully. But when you zoom out, that narrative arc is always going to be there as long as I stick to the intent of the project. A few practical things fall out of this. When I have already decided something from experience, refusing to commit to it isn't more open; that is just a performance. The balance sits in not over-structuring up front, but I can commit when things are obviously settled.

My workouts also weren't on point. Energy was pretty low. I gave myself a break because I was adjusting my sleep schedule, but I have to be better next week. Two fixes to try include drinking water right away upon waking, and adding a longer warm-up before diving in.

### Planning notes for W37

- Establish more structure and long-term planning from experience
- Keep writing momentum on the foundational piece and in general. Now, with other things to work on, it should feel even better.
- Two workout tweaks
- Keep an eye on whether I am really into the work or just doing it
